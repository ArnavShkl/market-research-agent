JioMausam · Elimination round · Draft 6 (locked) · 3-minute script (445 words, about 150 words a minute)

Slide 1  [0:00–0:17] India doesn’t have a weather-data problem. It has a weather-decision problem. On 26 July 2005, Santacruz got 944 millimetres of rain; Colaba, 20 kilometres away, got 73. A city-level alert can still tell a business only “heavy rain in Mumbai”. It needs to know which zones to act on.

Slide 2  [0:17–0:34] Jio may already own the infrastructure for a new weather-sensing layer. Rain weakens its microwave links, and the excess loss estimates rainfall along each link. JioMausam fuses those signals with radar and gauges into a weather grid, then into decision-ready alerts.

Slide 3  [0:34–0:56] Published science supports the physics, and has mapped rain across a whole country. We tested the method ourselves on 500 real links from a public dataset. The signal is real: 0.95 against radar. But the key finding was where it failed: links alone missed all three of our accuracy gates.

Slide 4  [0:56–1:14] So we never use links alone; accuracy decides the mix. Jio’s links, filtered by network-health data, plus IMD radar, gauges and satellite, with paid data only if it lifts accuracy. The pilot scores every mix against held-out gauges and picks the cheapest that clears every gate.

Slide 5  [1:14–1:30] Reliance is the first proving ground. The hero test is Jio’s own network team: can we tell rain from equipment faults before sending a crew? Jio owns the data and the decision. JioMart then tests commercial value; MyJio tests reach at scale.

Slide 6  [1:30–1:48] Then we sell the same intelligence to the market. Rain hits Zone A: JioMausam sends a zone alert, and the platform decides how to adjust dispatch, delivery times or incentives. Delivery comes first, then logistics, cities and insurers, then agriculture and power.

Slide 7  [1:48–2:04] Our edge isn’t the sensor; it’s integration. Jio senses, JioMausam understands, Reliance reaches customers, licensed partners protect. We sell intelligence outside and create savings inside Reliance; insurance is optional. So the business stands even if insurance never launches.

Slide 8  [2:04–2:22] Our original idea was Kavach. Stress-testing showed the stronger business is the intelligence underneath it. Insurance becomes one application: JioMausam supplies trigger data, and a licensed insurer would pay a fixed benefit, only after the accuracy and actuarial gates. If it never launches, nothing breaks.

Slide 9  [2:22–2:38] If we offer Kavach, Indian weather sets the price. From 25 years of IMD records: Basic about 45 rupees a month in Mumbai, 52 in Nagpur, 29 in Ahmedabad. Plus is priced from twelve historical heavy-rain days, capped at five paid days: 208.

Slide 10  [2:38–3:00] Don’t believe us; test it. Ninety days, three gates: coverage, accuracy, value. We don’t pre-select the city; the audit does. We aren’t asking Jio to build a weather network, but to discover what its network can tell us. First prove the signal. Then sell the intelligence. Protection comes last.

THE ONE IDEA TO LAND
  We don’t need to prove Jio has built a weather network. We need to prove its existing network holds enough
  extra weather information to create economic value. The 90-day pilot answers exactly that.

HOW TO TALK ABOUT OUR NUMBERS
  They are ours: our pipeline (open-source pycomlink, KIT) on pycomlink’s public dataset of 500 real links in Germany,
  10–20 May 2018, with German DWD radar as the reference. Tuned on 3 days, scored on 8 unseen days. Not Jio data.

  Links alone vs our accuracy gates (all measured against radar):
    Trigger events caught        68%  (1,120 of 1,639)   gate ≥80%   FAIL
    Rain totals                  18% low (bias 0.82)     gate ±10%   FAIL
    Our triggers not confirmed   18%  (244 of 1,364)     gate <15%   FAIL
  Q&A only: 0.88 typical single link · 0.78 map level · rain/no-rain days 91% detected, 5% false.

  Say:  “Published science supports the physics. We tested the method ourselves, and links alone failed our gates.”
  Never say: “We tested Jio’s network.” · “Studies show 68%.” · “Jio can already sense the weather.”

MEMORISE THESE ANSWERS
  0.95 vs 68%: “0.95 is the network-wide hourly match, where local errors cancel. At map level it’s 0.78, and a trigger
    test is harsher: 9 mm against an 11 mm trigger is a miss. Links also read 18% low.”
  ₹208: “₹208 isn’t priced from our 10-mm-in-3-hour prototype trigger. It uses 25 years of daily IMD heavy-rain history,
    capped at five paid days. The pilot measures the 3-hour trigger’s real frequency and recalibrates before launch.”
  Hyperlocal exists: “Exactly. BMC gauges and IMD radar are inputs we fuse. The pilot’s radar-plus-gauges-without-links
    run measures what Jio adds.”
  Rain fade: “It may. Then Gate 1 confirms cheaply what weather information is available; if not, the same telemetry
    is a new signal.”
  No rain in days 61–90: “The back-test replays archived monsoon events; the full live test runs in Mumbai’s 2027 monsoon.”
  Who decides payouts: “Jio only supplies trigger data. A licensed insurer owns the contract, rules and payout.”
  Eligibility: riders already active in the zone before the event began, under predefined rules (stops logging in to
    collect a payout).
