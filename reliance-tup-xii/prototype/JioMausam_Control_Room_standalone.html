The India layer: 25 years of IMD station records

Indian operators don’t publish link data, so the network method is proven on the open German dataset. This tab uses real Indian weather, 2000–2024 daily reports from IMD stations at Mumbai (Santacruz and Colaba), Nagpur and Ahmedabad (archived by NOAA), to answer two questions: is Indian rain really hyperlocal, and what should Kavach cost in each city?

What Kavach should cost, city by city

₹300 a payout, capped at 5 days a year, 60% of premiums paid back as payouts. Monthly price = expected payouts ÷ 0.6 ÷ 12.
The finding: one flat price can’t work. Mumbai’s risk is rain, Nagpur’s and Ahmedabad’s is heat. Kavach Basic covers each city’s rare extreme days for ₹29–52 a month. Kavach Plus, bought by delivery apps for their riders, covers every work-stopping day.

Data: pycomlink/OpenSense example dataset (500 commercial microwave links, 1-minute signal levels, 10–20 May 2018; coordinates anonymised by the provider). Reference: German Weather Service (DWD) RADOLAN-YW radar, path-averaged and gridded. Processing: open-source pycomlink pipeline (per-link rolling-std wet/dry detection, constant baseline, Schleiss 2013 wet-antenna correction, ITU k–R relation), one multiplicative calibration factor fitted on days 1–3 () and tested on days 4–11. Map field: inverse-distance interpolation on a ~4 km grid. Policyholders are simulated. Radar is a reference, not perfect truth.
