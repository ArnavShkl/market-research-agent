How this test works

  1. Real signals. 500 microwave links from a German mobile network, signal strength every minute, 10–20 May 2018. 7.9 million readings.
  2. Rain from signal loss. Each link runs the steps shown in tab B to turn signal loss into millimetres of rain.
  3. Fair tuning. One scale factor is fitted on the tuning days only. Every score on this page uses days the model never saw.
  4. Three comparisons. Along each link's path, on the 4 km map, and as pay or no-pay decisions per square per day.

Reading the payout numbers

A decision is one square on one day: pay or don't pay.

Agreement is high partly because most square-days are dry, which is why we also quote events caught and false triggers. Combining links with radar and gauges is how the pilot lifts events caught to 80%.

The steps, in order

  1. Read the signal every minute: power sent minus power received, in dB.
  2. Fill short gaps of up to 5 minutes with a straight line.
  3. Spot rain from the wobble: if the signal's variation over the surrounding hour is bigger than 1.12 × this link's usual level, the minute counts as wet.
  4. Hold a dry baseline through each wet spell, using the last 5 dry minutes.
  5. Remove the wet-antenna effect, up to 1.5 dB (Schleiss et al., 2013).
  6. Convert dB to mm per hour with the ITU-R standard formula for this link's frequency and length.
  7. Apply one scale factor (×1.17) fitted across the network on the tuning days only.
