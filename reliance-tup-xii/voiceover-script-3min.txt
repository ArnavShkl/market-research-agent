[0:00] SLIDE 1 · Hook
    India's biggest weather network already exists.
    It has lakhs of stations, it covers every district, and it's switched on right now.
    Nobody noticed — because we call it Jio.

[0:12] SLIDE 2 · Problem: Mumbai 2005
    Weather in India is hyperlocal, but our data isn't.
    On 26 July 2005, Santacruz in Mumbai got 944 millimetres of rain.
    Colaba, twenty kilometres away, got 73.
    About forty radars can't see that difference.
    The people who lose the most — riders, vendors, labourers — get no help,
    and insurance claims take months.

[0:37] SLIDE 3 · Insight
    Here's the insight.
    Rain weakens the radio links between towers —
    and how much it weakens tells you how hard it's raining, minute by minute.
    Smartphone batteries, averaged across a city, track air temperature.
    So every tower is a rain gauge. Every phone is a thermometer.
    We don't build sensors. We become the sensor.

[1:00] SLIDE 4 · Proof + Indian pricing
    Does it actually work? We built it.
    On 500 real microwave links, our rain readings matched national radar at 0.95.
    Our payout rule made the same decision as radar ninety-five percent of the time.
    Then we priced Kavach on twenty-five years of IMD records —
    rain cover for Mumbai, heat cover for Nagpur and Ahmedabad.

[1:24] SLIDE 5 · Product
    Meet JioMausam —
    a live, street-level weather map built from network data.
    On top of it: Mausam Kavach —
    you add it to your recharge, from twenty-nine rupees a month.
    When the network measures your city's extreme — 47 degrees in Ahmedabad, a deluge in Mumbai —
    three hundred rupees lands in your JioPay wallet within hours.
    No claim forms. No surveyor. No waiting.

[1:50] SLIDE 6 · Business
    The business has three layers.
    One: Kavach premiums — one percent of Jio's 500 million users is five million policies —
    around 170 crore rupees a year.
    Two: delivery apps buy Kavach Plus for their riders; insurers and states buy the data.
    Three: Reliance is customer zero — JioMart, New Energy and Jio itself.

[2:12] SLIDE 7 · Why Reliance
    Why only Reliance?
    A startup can't own the towers.
    An insurer can't reach 500 million people.
    No other telecom has a payments app, an insurance arm, a retail chain
    and a giga-scale energy business under one roof.
    Reliance has every layer.

[2:29] SLIDE 8 · Roadmap & close
    We start with a ninety-day pilot in Vidarbha and Ahmedabad, validated against IMD gauges.
    Then an IRDAI sandbox product. Then a national map.
    India won't adapt to climate change only by building new infrastructure.
    It'll adapt by listening to the infrastructure it already has.
    This is JioMausam. Different by design.
